# Fixed-function GPU units repurposed for general computation — GPU relevance verdict ledger

cluster: candidate taxonomy letter **Q** — "fixed-function GPU units repurposed for general computation"
scope: RT cores / BVH traversal units / rasterisation and ROP hardware / texture units, either
       (a) mapped onto by a general-purpose problem in software, or (b) extended or generalised in
       hardware; plus the rendering-pipeline microarchitecture papers that characterise and motivate them.
last_checked: 2026-09-18
knowledge_as_of: 2026-09-18

Verdicts are `CORE_GPU` / `RELATED_GPU` / `EXCLUDE` / `UNRESOLVED`.
Access states are `PUBLIC_FULLTEXT` / `CLOSED_ACCESS` / `PENDING_FULLTEXT` /
`PUBLIC_ARTIFACT_ONLY` / `ABSTRACT_ONLY`.
`evidence_read` records what was actually read. **A verdict reached from an abstract, an
artifact, or a census row says so and may NOT be used to justify a deep analysis.**

**Counterfactual answer** column records the sharp form for this cluster: *"would the contribution
survive if the RT / graphics fixed-function unit were replaced by ordinary SIMT cores?"*
`NO` = it would not; the rationale names what the fixed-function unit supplies.
`NO (census/abstract-level)` = the mechanism is evident from the confirmed title/abstract but the
paper was not read, so the answer is provisional.

**HW/SW** column is the cluster's spine: `HW` = proposes a microarchitectural change;
`SW` = a software mapping onto existing shipping hardware; `HW+SW` = both.

**S/M** column: `SIM` = simulator only; `MEAS` = real GPU silicon; `SIM+MEAS` = hybrid, explained
in the rationale. **Simulated area/energy figures are never reported as measured.**

**Tooling constraints that shaped access states** (do not re-derive): `dl.acm.org` → 403,
`ieeexplore.ieee.org` → 418, `dblp.org` → robots-blocked, `par.nsf.gov` → robots-blocked,
`arxiv.org/search` / `export.arxiv.org` / `web.archive.org` → unavailable. Several papers in this
cluster are genuinely open access on ACM DL but unreachable here; those are `PENDING_FULLTEXT`,
**not** `CLOSED_ACCESS`.

**Cross-cluster note.** *Heliostat: Harnessing Ray Tracing Accelerators for Page Table Walks*
(ISCA 2025) is adjudicated `CORE_GPU` / `ABSTRACT_ONLY` in `_LEDGER_memory_virtualization.md` and
is **not re-adjudicated here**. It is treated as context: it is the same mechanism as this cluster's
software branch (map a non-graphics traversal onto the RT unit) applied to a *system* structure
(the GPU page table) rather than an application data structure. See §4 for its place in the lineage.
Likewise **RoCC** (ISCA 2026) is owned by the communication cluster's ledger; it is adjudicated
below **only** as a fixed-function-repurposing data point, and that row is advisory, not its verdict of record.

## 4. Taxonomy finding — is "Q: fixed-function GPU units repurposed for general computation" a real category?

**Short answer: yes, but it must be defined by *mechanism*, and it has two branches plus a
supporting literature that must not be folded into it.**

### 4.1 The evidence that it is a real category

1. **The papers cite each other across the software/hardware divide, in both directions.**
   - `GPU-MICRO24-121` (HSU) cites Mandarapu et al. (= `GPU-ICS24-125`, Arkade), RTIndeX, RTNN,
     Zellmann, Morrical, Wald, Evangelou as the reformulate-as-ray-casting predecessors it exists
     to replace `[paper]`.
   - `GPU-PPoPP25-128` (LibRTS) cites **both** MICRO 2024 hardware papers by name and positions
     them as complementary hardware research `[paper]`.
   - `GPU-ASPLOS25-124` (Treelet) closes with: "With proposals from Ha et al. [14] and Barnes et
     al. [6] that extend ray tracing accelerators in GPUs to support more general tree traversal
     workloads, we believe the hardware modifications and treelet queue optimizations in this work
     [could] accelerate general tree traversal workloads on GPUs as well" `[paper]`.
   - `GPU-ISCA25-127` (CoopRT), a pure graphics paper, cites both MICRO 2024 papers `[paper]`.
   - **TTP (ISCA 2026) enumerates the category explicitly** under a "non-graphics RT-core uses"
     heading: Heliostat, LibRTS, TTA, Arkade `[paper]`. A prefetcher paper listing these four
     together is the strongest single piece of evidence that the category is legible from inside
     the RT-architecture community rather than imposed from outside.

2. **The same mechanism recurs on three different fixed-function units.**
   - **RT core** — HSU, TTA, Arkade, LibRTS, RT-BarnesHut, RTSpMSpM, X-HD, Heliostat.
   - **ROP / rasteriser / stencil** — VR-Pipe (stencil MSB as a termination flag), RoCC (ROP for
     tensor collectives), Gaussian Blending Unit.
   - **Texture unit** — DEFCON ("GPU Texture Hardware" for deformable convolution), TMModel.
   If the category were really "RT cores", the ROP and texture instances would be unexplained.

3. **It has a stable internal structure — the HW/SW split — that predicts the papers' shape.**
   Software-mapping papers (Arkade, LibRTS, X-HD, RT-BarnesHut, RTSpMSpM) are **measured on real
   silicon**, report large speedups against SIMT baselines, and spend their pages on *encodings*
   and on working around the unit's opacity. Hardware papers (HSU, TTA, CoopRT, Treelet, TTP,
   VR-Pipe) are **simulated**, report modest single-digit-to-2× speedups, and spend their pages on
   *which existing functional units can be reused*. This is not a coincidence of topic; it is a
   consequence of the category's defining constraint (the unit is fixed, so you either bend the
   problem or you open the unit).

### 4.2 Why it is **not** a sub-case of A or B

It is not a topic about a workload (so not a sparse/irregular or a rendering category) and not a
topic about a resource (so not a memory or communication category). Its members share **no
application domain** — k-NN, spatial joins, Barnes–Hut, SpMSpM, Hausdorff distance, page-table
walks, tensor collectives, deformable convolution — and **no resource**. What they share is a
*method*: take a unit whose function is frozen in silicon for graphics, and obtain general-purpose
value from it either by reformulating the problem into its one predicate or by widening the
predicate. Filing these under their applications would scatter them across five clusters and lose
exactly the thing they have in common.

The strongest structural confirmation is the **exact parallel with the tensor-core cluster**
(`_LEDGER_tensor_cores.md`): FlashSparse and Acc-SpMM reshape sparse algebra to fit the MMA
instruction's fixed operand extents with **no hardware change**, precisely as Arkade and LibRTS
reshape search to fit the ray-AABB predicate. That parallel is an argument for a *cross-cutting
method axis*, not for merging the two — the units, the constraints and the communities differ.

**Two lineage findings worth recording precisely:**

- **Heliostat belongs to this line, on the evidence.** Its mechanism is the software branch's move
  (map a non-graphics multi-level traversal onto the RT unit) applied to the page table; its
  co-author **Won Woo Ro also co-authors TTA**; and **TTP independently lists it beside LibRTS,
  TTA and Arkade under non-graphics RT-core uses** `[paper, TTP]`. That is three independent
  attachments. The caveat is that its *verdict of record* sits in the memory-virtualisation ledger,
  which is correct — it is simultaneously a GPU-virtual-memory paper. **It is the clearest evidence
  that category Q cross-cuts the existing clusters rather than replacing them.**
- **The RT branch and the rasterisation branch do not cite each other.** VR-Pipe cites **no**
  RT-core repurposing work at all `[paper — verified by targeted query]`, and no RT paper read here
  cites VR-Pipe, RoCC or DEFCON. The category is therefore **real in mechanism but not yet
  self-aware across units**. Recording that honestly matters: a survey that asserts a unified
  "fixed-function repurposing" community would be overstating what the citations support.

### 4.4 Recommended category definition and boundaries

> **Q — Fixed-function GPU units repurposed for general computation.** Work whose contribution is
> obtaining non-original-purpose value from a GPU unit whose function is fixed in silicon for
> graphics (RT/BVH traversal units, rasteriser, ROP/blend, Z/stencil, texture, video CODEC),
> either by reformulating a general-purpose problem into that unit's fixed predicate (**software
> mapping**) or by widening the predicate at minimum marginal area (**hardware generalisation**).

**In:** HSU, TTA, Arkade, LibRTS, RT-BarnesHut, RTSpMSpM, X-HD, VR-Pipe, RoCC, DEFCON, TMModel,
Heliostat (cross-listed), Rethinking Collision Detection; GauTracer and GRTX if their baseline is
confirmed to be a GPU RT core.

**Out, and why:**
- **LIBRA, CoopRT, Treelet, TTP, RayN** — graphics for graphics' sake. Keep them as a labelled
  **sub-collection "Q0: rendering-pipeline characterisation"**, because the repurposing papers'
  central claims (the RT unit tolerates divergence; the ROP is the scaling limit) are *only*
  quantified here. Treelet's 0.37 SIMT efficiency and CoopRT's 30–70% idle lanes are the numbers
  that keep the repurposing literature honest.
- **GSCore, Gaussian Blending Unit and the dedicated 3DGS accelerators** — they *build* hardware
  rather than repurpose it. GSCore is the value contrast (it beats VR-Pipe on performance and loses
  on generality, by VR-Pipe's own admission), not a member.
- **ARC and Interleaved Bitstream Execution** — SIMT-pipeline mechanisms wearing rendering or
  irregular-workload clothing. Route to the atomics/SIMT and sparse/irregular clusters.
- **CLM and the ASPLOS 2026 offloading papers** — GPU memory management.

**One caution for whoever writes the synthesis.** This cluster's headline numbers are systematically
non-comparable: the software branch measures real silicon (RTX 3090, RTX 4060 Ti) against SIMT
baselines and reports 10×–100×; the hardware branch simulates (Accel-Sim V100, Vulkan-Sim 8–30 SM
scale models, Emerald) against an *already-RT-accelerated* baseline and reports 1.1×–2.5×. They are
not measuring the same thing, and no table should place them in the same column. The two MICRO 2024
papers are not even modelling the same RT hardware — **HSU generalises an AMD RDNA3-style datapath
where software owns the traversal stack; TTA generalises an NVIDIA-style RTA where hardware owns
it** — so even those two must not be compared directly.
