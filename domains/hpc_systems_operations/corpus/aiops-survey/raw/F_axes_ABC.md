## A.2 Strongest existing method
**Kaleidoscope (A3, SC'20)** — it is the only work in the HPC literature that completes anomaly → component localization → failure-mode classification → likely cause, validated on **843 real production issues over 2 years**, with 99.3%/95.8% accuracy.
**What it does not do:** it is scoped to *one subsystem* (the distributed storage stack). Its model hierarchy is hand-built from domain knowledge of that subsystem. It carries no GPU, no fabric-switch, no scheduler-policy, and no facility signal. It does not emit an operator action. It has never been shown to port to another system or another subsystem. **This is the paper that most nearly closes Axis A and it closes only a slice of it.**

Runner-up on *breadth*: **PACE (A4)** is the only HPC-venue work doing explicit causal discovery across cooling/power/network — but it's a workshop paper, uses Granger (which A15 empirically shows collapses on short real telemetry windows), and never touches job performance.

## A.3 Closest production implementation
What operators actually run today = **LDMS/OVIS + DCDB-Wintermute + Kafka(STREAM)/Prometheus → Grafana dashboards + threshold alerts + human ticket triage**, plus per-site bespoke scripts. Prodigy/E2EWatch (Sandia) and NodeSentry (NG-Tianhe) are the only ML detectors actually wired into an operator console. ORNL's EPIC is the only LLM-assisted operator interface in production, and it does descriptive/predictive analytics — **not RCA**. No HPC center runs a cross-layer causal RCA system.

## A.4 Remaining difference (concrete + testable)
> Given a degraded job on a GPU supercomputer, no published system produces a **ranked attribution over layers** — {this node's CPU/memory, this job's GPU, the fabric (which links/switch group), Lustre (which OST/MDT), the scheduler's placement decision, the CDU/power envelope} — that is (a) **validated against operator-confirmed incident tickets**, (b) survives the small-sample regime where classical causal discovery is known to score 0% (A15), and (c) terminates in an action from a closed set an operator can actually take {drain node, requeue+replace nodes, reroute/replace link, throttle a top-user's I/O, change placement policy, raise inlet temp, do nothing}.

Three concrete sub-gaps, each independently testable:
1. **Layer-identifiability.** Is the blamed layer even identifiable from the telemetry a center collects? Nobody has published an identifiability/confusion analysis. Testable: build a confusion matrix of layer attribution over injected faults per layer, at production scale.
2. **Attribution under confounding by placement.** Slurm placement confounds node health and fabric locality. Nobody conditions on the scheduler. Testable and unique to HPC (cloud RCA has no equivalent).
3. **Action-value, not explanation-value.** Every HPC paper evaluates F1/top-k. Nobody evaluates *regret of the operator action taken*. CENTILE (B) is the only work anywhere in this space that evaluates a telemetry model "by the decisions it drives".

## A.5 VERDICT — **PARTIALLY_ADDRESSED** (detection: CLOSED; localization: PARTIALLY_ADDRESSED; causal attribution: STILL_OPEN; operator action: STILL_OPEN)

Detection at production scale is **closed** — SC'23 (Prodigy) and SC'25 (NodeSentry) both landed. Do **not** propose a detector.

## A.6 Would this be an SC Technical Paper?
**Yes, but only in the causal-attribution/action framing, and it is the highest-risk of the three.**

Reviewers would demand:
- ≥1 production GPU system, **≥6–12 months** of continuous multi-layer telemetry; ideally 2 systems.
- **≥50–100 operator-confirmed incidents** with ticket-level ground truth (this is the binding constraint — Kaleidoscope's 843 issues is the bar that has been set at SC).
- Baselines: **Prodigy, NodeSentry, PACE/Granger, Kaleidoscope-style hierarchy**, plus **at least one microservice RCA method ported** (RCD/CausalRCA/Sage-class) — reviewers from outside HPC will ask this and it is the #1 way to be desk-rejected on novelty.
- Metrics: top-1/top-3 layer attribution accuracy, time-to-attribution, **and an action-regret or MTTR-delta measure**, plus a false-attribution cost analysis.
- An ablation showing *which* layer's telemetry actually carries the attribution signal.

**Strongest reviewer counterargument to novelty:** *"Cross-layer, multi-modal RCA with causal graphs is a mature literature in cloud/microservices (CauseInfer 2014, Sage ASPLOS'21, CausalRCA, RCD NeurIPS'22, CIRCA KDD'22). Kaleidoscope did it for HPC storage at SC'20. PACE did causal discovery on datacenter telemetry at SC'25W. The authors have changed the domain, not the method."*
**Your only defensible answers:** (i) HPC-specific confounding by the scheduler + tightly-coupled collectives, which breaks the microservice trace-graph assumption entirely (there are no request traces in HPC — this is a *real* structural difference and you should lead with it); (ii) an operator-validated incident corpus at a scale nobody outside DOE has published; (iii) an identifiability negative result. A pipeline composition will not pass.

## A.7 Testable with KISTI telemetry?
**Yes, with one hard dependency.** Slurm accounting ✓, DCGM ✓, CPU/mem ✓, Lustre stats ✓ (client + server-side jobstats), power ✓, CDU ✓, syslog/RAS ✓. **Slingshot fabric counters are the risk** — note that Bhatele's group explicitly could not get Rosetta switch counters on Perlmutter/Frontier (A5). If KISTI can get per-port/per-switch Slingshot telemetry (HPE STT / fabric manager), that is itself a differentiator versus the strongest competing group. **The real blocker is not telemetry — it is operator-confirmed incident labels.** Secure ops-team commitment to ticket labeling *before* committing to this axis.

- CENTILE: does not consider telemetry cost; "across domains" is not "across GPU generations"; preprint, scale unpinned.

## B.4 Remaining difference (concrete + testable)
> **No published work measures the transfer penalty of a numeric-telemetry operational model across (a) two different HPC sites, (b) two GPU generations, (c) two fabric generations, or (d) a telemetry schema change.** The only measured generalization number in the HPC numeric-telemetry literature is **0.898 → 0.726 F1 across nodes of a single system** (B7, a 3-page workshop paper).

Testable claims nobody has made:
1. **Quantify the transfer penalty matrix.** Train on {M100 (V100/IB-HDR), Fugaku (A64FX/Tofu-D), Eclipse (CPU), KISTI Nurion (KNL/OPA), KISTI Hanggang (H100-class/Slingshot)} and cross-evaluate. Report ΔF1, Δdetection-delay, and #target-samples-to-recover.
2. **Isolate *which* shift hurts.** Decompose the penalty into (i) hardware-generation shift, (ii) schema shift, (iii) workload-mix shift, (iv) scheduler-policy shift. Nobody has done this decomposition for any systems domain, let alone HPC. This is the most SC-flavored part of the axis.
3. **Test whether physically-grounded invariant features close the gap.** Hypothesis: raw counters don't transfer, but *dimensionless* derived quantities (utilization ratios, bytes/flop, stall fraction, power/TDP, queue-depth normalized by capacity) do. Cheap to test, and produces a usable negative result either way.
4. **Cost of maintenance, not just accuracy.** SE venues own "when to retrain" (B12/B15). Nobody has priced it in GPU-hours/year for an HPC center.

## C.2 Strongest existing method
Two, in different worlds:
- **For formal cost-vs-utility:** **PINT (C2)** and **Sonata (C1)**. PINT gives a hard per-packet bit budget with *proven* bounds; Sonata gives an ILP that partitions a telemetry query under real hardware resource constraints and achieves 3–7 orders of magnitude reduction with dynamic refinement.
  **What they do not do:** their utility function is a *declarative query with exact or analytically-boundable semantics* over a *packet stream* on a *programmable dataplane*. HPC operational telemetry has none of these: fixed-schema periodic counters, no programmable collection substrate, and a utility that is a **learned, non-analytic downstream detector**. Their cost is switch memory/stages/per-packet bits; the HPC cost is **collector CPU + network + long-term storage** (20 PB/5 yr, C7). **Transferability of the mechanisms is low; transferability of the *formulation* is high — and that is the honest read.**
- **For production cost-vs-benefit optimization:** **SuperBench's Selector (C13)**. It is the only deployed system that explicitly optimizes observation cost against detection benefit at 100k-GPU scale. **What it does not do:** it optimizes *active benchmark scheduling* (cost = stolen GPU-time), not passive telemetry collection (cost = bytes). It is cloud, not HPC.

## C.3 Closest production implementation
**LDMS/DCDB + Kafka(STREAM)/Prometheus + Cassandra/Elasticsearch, with cost controlled by three blunt levers:** (1) fixed sampling interval (5–10 s, occasionally 1 s), (2) in-memory pre-aggregation before storage (SuperMUC-NG: 700k → 60k inserts/s, ~11.7×), (3) retention thinning to aggregates (C14, and DEEP-EST's 1-year TTL). **All three levers are set by engineering judgment. None is tied to a measured downstream-quality constraint.** ORNL's own reduction strategy is described as *"identifying data fields that either provide no useful information"* (C7) — i.e., manual.

## C.4 Remaining difference (concrete + testable)
> **Nobody has published, for any HPC system, a Pareto frontier of telemetry cost against downstream operational quality.** Every reduction result in the HPC literature is a *modelling-side* dimensionality reduction (C10 3014→82, C11 806→156, C12 −57%, C9 ~700→60) evaluated only by "accuracy did not drop." No paper varies **sampling interval**, no paper varies **retention resolution**, no paper reports **detection delay** as a function of telemetry rate, and no paper measures the **quality cost of reduction on RCA** (as opposed to detection).

Four gaps, all testable, in increasing order of ambition:
1. **The measurement gap (easy, high-value, currently unfilled).** C6 asserts 1 Hz monitoring has "no statistically significant adverse effects" **without publishing a measurement**. A rigorous monitoring-induced-perturbation study on a modern GPU system — across sampling rates, with tightly-coupled collectives and jitter-sensitive apps, at scale — does not exist in the literature. This alone is a solid ISC/Cluster/MODA paper and a necessary component of an SC paper.
2. **The detection-delay gap.** Detection latency is the metric operators actually care about (a node killed 10 min late costs 10 min × N nodes). **Not one HPC AD paper reports F1 as a function of sampling interval.** NodeSentry reports 36 ms/point inference but never asks what happens at 60 s vs 15 s vs 1 s collection.
3. **The detection-vs-diagnosis asymmetry (the sharpest claim available to you).** Hypothesis: **detection is cheap and diagnosis is expensive** — a small, coarse metric subset suffices to *detect*, but *localizing which layer is to blame* requires metrics and resolution that detection discards. If true, this invalidates every "we pruned 97% of metrics and F1 held" result as an argument for reducing what is *collected*, because those pruned metrics are exactly what RCA needs. **I found no paper anywhere — HPC, cloud, or networking — that tests this.** It is a crisp, falsifiable, mechanism-level claim.
4. **The joint formulation.** minimize bytes(metric-subset **×** sampling-rate **×** retention-tier) s.t. detection F1 ≥ τ, detection delay ≤ δ, RCA top-k localization ≥ ρ. Submodular/greedy or Lagrangian, with a **tiered** answer (collect-always at low rate; burst to high rate on trigger; retain full resolution only around incidents) — i.e., the HPC analogue of Sonata's dynamic refinement, but triggered by a learned detector instead of a query predicate.

> **Does telemetry reduction that preserves anomaly *detection* quality destroy cross-layer *diagnosis* quality — and does an optimized telemetry subset transfer across HPC generations, or must it be re-derived per system?**

This is A×B×C. It is falsifiable, it produces a publishable result whichever way it comes out, it requires exactly the telemetry a large center already collects, and its baselines (NodeSentry, Prodigy, Gorilla, Sonata-refinement, MAML-transfer) all exist and are runnable. No paper I found asks it.
