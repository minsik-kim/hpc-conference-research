### 9. The 5–8 strongest SC-regular precedents

Ranked by how completely each turns an operational problem into a generalizable systems research contribution.

1. **Live Forensics for HPC Systems / Kaleidoscope — SC20 (Jha, Iyer et al., UIUC/NCSA).** The template. Real operational pain (failures nobody can localize) → a near-real-time hierarchical-ML detection+localization+diagnosis framework → deployed on Blue Waters → validated against **843 real production incidents** with 99.3%/95.8% numbers and <0.01% overhead. It has everything reviewers ask for: production data, deployment, quantified operational outcome, and a method that is described generally enough to port.
2. **Prodigy — SC23 (Aksar, Coskun et al., BU + Sandia).** The best precedent for *unsupervised* anomaly detection specifically. Its research contribution is the framing — "labels don't exist in production, so build a method that doesn't need them" — and it reports both an offline score (0.95 F1) and a **production deployment number (88%)**. This is exactly the shape a KISTI 한강 anomaly-detection paper should take. Its 2025 successor (NodeSentry) proves the line is still live and still accepting.
3. **Fine-grained Automated Failure Management — SC25 (Intel + Argonne, on Aurora).** The only **L7/D5** paper in the census: a real closed loop that takes automated repair action in production, with a headline operational result (**MTTR ↓ up to 84×**). It proves SC will publish a system that *acts*, not just one that *detects* — and it does so with a meta-database + multi-strike policy design that is genuinely reusable. If KISTI wants to argue for closed-loop remediation, this is the citation.
4. **GPU Lifetimes on Titan — SC20 (Ostrouchov et al., ORNL)** and its 2025 successor **Story of Two GPUs — SC25 (Cui, Iyer et al., UIUC/NCSA).** Treat these as one lineage. Both take a pure operational data asset (100,000 GPU-years; 11.7M GPU-hours) and extract a *scientific* result — Titan: GPU lifetime is governed by cooling geometry and job placement; H100 vs A100: the newer GPU's memory is **3.2× worse** per-GPU MTBE and ~5% overprovisioning is required. The generalizable move is applying proper statistics (survival analysis, MTBE, availability projection) rather than descriptive plots, plus **public data release**. This is the most directly imitable pattern for a national center that owns years of GPU error logs.
5. **Cost-Aware Prediction of Uncorrected DRAM Errors in the Field — SC20 (BSC).** The reason to elevate this above other prediction papers: it makes the *evaluation methodology* the contribution. It argues that F1/precision are the wrong objective for an operational predictor and replaces them with a cost model denominated in lost node-hours (57% reduction, 21,000 node-hours/yr). Any KISTI paper proposing prediction should adopt this framing — it converts "our classifier is good" into "our operations are cheaper."
6. **A Taxonomy of Error Sources in HPC I/O Machine Learning Models — SC22 (Isakov, ANL/LBNL/ASU).** The contrarian precedent, and undervalued. It is a *negative-results and epistemics* paper: multi-year logs from two systems, asking why deployed I/O ML models fail, producing a five-mode failure taxonomy plus diagnostics for attributing your own model's error. For a group entering this field, this is the highest-leverage kind of paper to write — it needs data and honesty rather than a novel algorithm, and it generalizes by construction.
